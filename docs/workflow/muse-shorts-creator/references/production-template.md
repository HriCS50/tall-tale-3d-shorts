# Production brief template

Use this as Muse's working production document. Fill all fields; leave no placeholders. The same document supplies the complete prompt-only result and the executable creative brief in create mode. Choose the requested mode using SKILL.md, never from a heading inside this template.

In create mode, show or attach the filled brief and proceed to actual production; the document is not a substitute for a requested video. In prompt-only mode, return it in one fenced text block, with only the initial style menu if applicable and the required series continuation question outside. Instructions about rendering inside a prompt-only brief describe the future production; do not execute them until the user requests creation.

The timestamps below demonstrate the notation only. Set actual scene durations, spoken windows, pauses, and B-roll intervals for the user's story. After producing audio/video, update the brief with measured timing. A narrator is optional: for dialogue-only stories, list just the speaking characters. Keep directions outside quoted speech.

```text
MUSE SHORT PRODUCTION BRIEF
Title: [specific title]
Episode: [i of N, for a series only]
Create a finished [D]-second vertical animated short using the following brief.
Format: 9:16, 1080 × 1920, 30 fps. Language: [language].
Audience and intended feeling: [specific audience, emotional journey].

STORY AND PAYOFF DIRECTION
Opening viewer question: [one concrete question or anticipated result].
Progression: [the specific causal changes, attempt, or discovery].
Payoff: [what this episode answers or makes the audience feel/understand].
[If useful: original setup detail, when it returns, and its changed meaning.]
Use the selected visual style below; engagement comes from the story's
clarity, consequences, and payoff, not imitation of a reference creator.

STYLE AND VISUAL CONTINUITY
[Selected style name plus its usable visual rules: palette, materials,
character design, setting, lighting, camera grammar, caption treatment.]
[Recurring character IDs, appearance, proportions, clothing/accessories,
key props, geography, and time-of-day progression.]
[For a series, enough context for this episode to work without prior chats.]

CAST AND VOICE DIRECTION
[NARRATOR: timbre/register, cadence, personality, emotional arc.]
[CHAR_A and CHAR_B, when present: distinct voices, stable assignments,
their relationship, and line-specific emotional intentions.]
Switch to the assigned voice at every speaker change; keep identities
consistent. Make delivery conversational and emotionally responsive, with
natural micro-pauses, varied emphasis, restrained breaths and reactions.
Read only the quoted spoken words. Do not speak timestamps, speaker IDs,
pause markers, scene IDs, or performance notes.

TIMED SCRIPT
Times are target performance windows including all marked pauses.
[S01 | 00:00.00–00:03.50 | NARRATOR | curious, inviting]
[00:00.00–00:01.70] "[exact spoken phrase]"
[00:01.70–00:01.95] [silent thought pause: 0.25 seconds]
[00:01.95–00:03.50] "[exact spoken phrase]"
[Continue with actual timestamps, speaker turns, delivery cues, dialogue,
and purposeful reaction/silence. Every interval must fit its scene.]

SCENE-BY-SCENE VIDEO TIMELINE
[S01 | same 00:00.00–00:03.50 range]
Viewer change: [what this scene adds or why its intentional hold matters].
Attention target: [the one action, face, object, or detail to notice first].
A-roll [absolute interval]: [framing, setting, visible character action,
expression, camera move, change from start to end].
B-roll [absolute interval inside the scene]: [specific insert/cutaway,
its action, what it explains, transition in/out; state which speaker's
audio continues over it or whether a reaction/sound takes over].
Text/captions: [exact essential label, caption chunks and emphasis].
Audio: [ambience, specific effect/reaction and timestamp or word trigger].
Continuity/transition: [object attachment/position, match cut or transition].
[For clue/reveal scenes: what is visible now, what becomes visible later,
its exact action/word trigger, and a brief readable reaction/landing beat.]
[Repeat for every scene. If a scene has no B-roll, omit that field; include
purposeful B-roll where the story benefits across the complete video.]

CAPTIONS, MUSIC, AND MIX
[Selected style's typography, chunk length, emphasis and placement.]
Synchronize captions to the final spoken audio, not a guessed reading speed.
Keep the caption zone and key visuals clear of platform controls.
[Music mood and progression; where it dips, changes, or falls silent.]
Keep voices intelligible and natural; duck music and effects under speech.
Preserve micro-pauses and reaction beats. Avoid harsh effects and clipping.

TIMING AND FINISHING
Treat the above as a complete [D]-second edit including the ending hold.
Time the voice first, align cuts and captions to the final audio, and keep
B-roll within its assigned scene windows. If synthesis overruns, trim
nonessential wording or adjust within these windows; do not speed-read away
the pauses or silently exceed the requested duration.
Check individual dialogue windows after subtracting pauses and silent
action; a comfortable whole-video average must not hide rushed lines.
[Specific ending action, final spoken payoff, sound tail and visual hold.]

RENDER AND QUALITY CHECK
Render the finished MP4 with voice, music, effects and captions. Provide a
clear 9:16 cover image from the strongest story beat: [specified moment].
Before delivery, inspect the complete edit and listen to the final mix.
Check voice switches, natural pauses, B-roll relevance and timing, caption
sync, prop use, character continuity, readable dark scenes, and overlay
collisions. Verify that important evidence stays legible, B-roll reveals
facts in the intended order, and the ending pays the opening promise.
Fix issues before claiming QA is complete. Report actual export
details and any unmet requirement honestly. If a required capability is
unavailable, identify it and propose a concrete substitute.

FACTUAL PRODUCTION NOTES [only when applicable]
[Concise verified claims with supporting source URLs, uncertainty, and any
dramatization labels; these notes are not spoken or shown as captions.]
```
