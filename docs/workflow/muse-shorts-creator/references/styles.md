# Style direction for Muse

These six creative treatments are fully specified here. Apply the selected treatment to the production brief and the actual video. Durations are defaults, not limits. Expensive or ambitious treatments are welcome; select for the idea, not for low rendering cost.

## Deep Dive 3D

- **Default:** 30–45 seconds; curious, cinematic science and “what happens if” stories.
- **Look:** sculpted stylized people, warm sunlit environments, shallow focus, soft shadows. For body topics, move from the real world into a cyan translucent x-ray body, then macro interiors, and back out. For other science, use analogous exterior/cutaway/macro scales instead of inventing irrelevant organs.
- **Camera/action:** deliberate push-ins, orbits, or traveling macro shots; meaningful motion in every shot. Keep physical and cutaway representations coherent. Show danger with diagrams, glow, or warning color rather than gore.
- **B-roll:** mechanism close-ups, organ or material details, particles, comparisons, labels attached to the feature being explained.
- **Voice/audio:** warm, curious conversational narrator; wonder before the reveal, a small pause to let a visual discovery register. Low atmospheric pulse or light music depending on stakes. Character dialogue uses separate voices.
- **Captions:** 1–3 words, white with a dark outline, one yellow emphasis word. Labels remain separate from captions and clear of the subject.

## Night File 3D

- **Default:** 30–45 seconds; quiet, uneasy investigation.
- **Look:** dark 3D environments with one motivated key light; faceless silhouettes, a restrained red subject treatment, cream typewriter text, subtle grain. Reserve red for the subject and evidence annotations. Keep enough edge light to read the action.
- **Camera/action:** slow push-ins and drifts, typically 3–5 second shots; carefully motivated hard cuts. Avoid turning darkness into an unreadable empty frame.
- **B-roll:** verified dates and locations, case-file pages, evidence details, maps, clocks, empty spaces, archival-style inserts clearly labeled when reconstructed.
- **Voice/audio:** intimate, measured investigator; varied emphasis and well-placed quiet, not a monotone whisper. Low drone, room tone, one restrained impact for a reveal. Never invent testimony to obtain a voice switch.
- **Captions:** 2–5 words in cream typewriter lettering, one red keyword; readable typing animation. Fact and date overlays must be accurate.
- **Story:** event and evidence lead to what is known and what remains unresolved. Do not fabricate a mystery ending for a solved case.

## Inside Out

- **Default:** 25–40 seconds; fast, friendly 2D explanation.
- **Look:** clean flat vector-like shapes, simple expressive hero, warm off-white backdrop, pink organs, cyan x-ray views, red warnings, yellow emphasis. No photoreal lighting or 3D drift. Use a coherent whole-body x-ray mode.
- **Camera/action:** 1.5–3.5 second beats with punch-ins, pans, diagram changes, and reactions; a sentence may flow over multiple shots.
- **B-roll:** mechanism cutaways, objects moving through a diagram, magnified details, before/after comparisons, counters or simple arrows that explain rather than decorate.
- **Voice/audio:** approachable and curious, with a little surprise and a natural pause before correcting a misconception. Light rhythmic music and soft illustrative effects.
- **Captions:** 1–3 bold words, white with dark outline and one yellow keyword. Use the spoken wording, with sensible display forms for numbers.

## Stick Story

- **Default:** 30–45 seconds; tense, clever, lightly funny scenarios.
- **Look:** black stick figures with large expressive white eyes, flat colorful environments, consistent location and props. At night give figures a soft warm edge so they remain visible. Gold step cards, red danger marks, green successful-action checks.
- **Camera/action:** usually 1.5–3 second visual beats; eyes react before the body. Mix establishing shots, action shots, reactions, and clear prop close-ups. Avoid holding the same wide composition throughout.
- **B-roll:** phone display, equipment inventory, route diagrams, environmental clues, hand/prop interactions, changing daylight. Show an explanatory inset as an inset, not as an unexplained floating physical prop.
- **Voice/audio:** lively, human and reassuring, with thought pauses and restrained comic reactions. Distinct character voices when multiple characters speak. Soft pops for cards and occasional rounded effects; no constant transition noise.
- **Captions:** sentence case, normally 2–4 words on a dark rounded bar, one gold word. Allow a short grammatical phrase if still comfortably readable.
- **Story:** situation → mistaken impulse/consequence → useful steps → payoff. Verify safety advice for the actual setting; never imply a guaranteed rescue.

## Paper Atlas

- **Default:** 30–45 seconds; curious, expansive history.
- **Look:** layered cut-paper forms on aged parchment, visible paper edges and soft shadows, period silhouettes, simplified accurate maps, crimson routes, gold date ribbons and seals. A navy wash conveys night.
- **Camera/action:** map establishes where, date establishes when, then people and events; paper layers slide, unfold, reveal, and parallax. Show consequences with places and objects rather than graphic violence.
- **B-roll:** map zooms, route drawing, dated records, period objects, building details, timeline inserts. Avoid invented coastlines or quotations presented as historical evidence.
- **Voice/audio:** warm documentary storyteller, unhurried thought groups and gentle changes of emphasis; use the user's preferred accent if provided. Subtle paper movement, stamp sounds, and suitable period-inspired instrumental music.
- **Captions:** 2–4 words in a readable serif on torn-paper strips, one crimson emphasis word; gold is reserved for dates, crowns, and seals.

## Tall Tale 3D

- **Default:** 40–60 seconds; playful stories with an ingenious or absurd turn.
- **Look:** flat 2D cartoon people with thick ink outlines and large round heads in a sunny, saturated 3D world. Characters keep consistent distinguishing accessories and cast believable shadows. Do not turn the people into sculpted 3D characters or flatten the environment.
- **Camera/action:** moving camera in each shot, usually 1.5–3 second cuts; visible facial reactions, a punch-in on the reveal, a small reaction hold before the punchline.
- **B-roll:** objects central to the scheme, signs, receipts, transport, hands performing the trick, reaction close-ups, wide location inserts. The comic mechanism must remain understandable.
- **Voice/audio:** upbeat storyteller with conversational rewinds and comic timing, not nonstop speed. Give speaking characters distinct recurring voices. Light bouncy music, selective plops, stamps, dings, or boings; leave room for the joke to land.
- **Captions:** one word at a time, with tiny grammatical pairs allowed; bold white with dark edge, no colored keyword highlight.
- **Truth:** verify a story presented as true. An explicitly fictional user idea may use this visual style, but label the story as fiction and never market it as a true event.
